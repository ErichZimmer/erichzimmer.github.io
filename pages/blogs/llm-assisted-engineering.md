# Introduction
Ever since the publication of the transformer archetecture (Vaswani et al., 2017), large language models (LLMs) and small language models (SLMs) have taken the world at storm. Unlike previous recurrent models, transformers allow for more favorable scailing due to parallel processing capabilities which make better use of the avaiable resources. Through many iterations of models throughout the years, these generative AI (genAI) models have been refined through many years of rigorous research enabling challenging tasks to be automated like never before. As of late October of 2026, one has many powerful models available both in an open-weight, local level and propietary frontier cloud level. With these numerous options literally at anyone's fingertips, the use of LLMs and SLMs for automating workflows have been increasing at a significant rate. As such, reported blunders from the use of these tools are also increasing, as per reported incidents on LinkedIn, conference calls, and news media. To reduce such occurrences, some practices the author has experienced and developed are discussed in this blog.
